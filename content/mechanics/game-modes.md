# Game Modes

Game Modes alter emphasis, pacing, pressure, and genre presentation without replacing Arkenfell's established canon or game state. They are organized here in the preferred order: campaign modes, story structure, pace, then narration genre.

## Arkenfell and Campaign Modes

### Default — Medium
The baseline Arkenfell experience: grounded fantasy adventure, character-driven scenes, politics, mystery, danger, and natural pacing.

### Slice of Life — Very Easy
A gentle Arkenfell run focused on ordinary life, community, work, crafting, friendship, family, romance, settlement routines, and the small comforts and frictions that make a place feel lived in.

### Radiant Noble Fantasy — Easy
A heightened anime noble-fantasy mode with status reversal, magical talent, court rivals, mentors, academy energy, elegant danger, trauma-aware support, and hopeful adventure.

### Radiant Noble Fantasy — Medium
A noble-fantasy anime mode with status reversal, magical talent, court rivals, mentors, academy pressure, elegant danger, trauma-aware support, and meaningful consequences.

### Radiant Noble Fantasy — Hard
A harder noble-fantasy anime mode with status reversal, magical talent, court rivals, mentors, academy trials, elegant danger, trauma-aware support, faction pressure, and lasting consequences.

### Adventure — Medium
A balanced fantasy adventure mode with exploration, ruins, dangerous roads, faction contracts, mysteries, monsters, and regional conflicts.

### Exploration & Discovery — Medium
A player-led exploration mode focused on travel, ruins, forgotten roads, field investigation, geography, old traces, natural wonder, and discoveries earned by looking closely rather than by being handed a quest.

### Court Intrigue — Medium
A political mode focused on nobles, royal legitimacy, succession, secrets, alliances, reputation, etiquette, and social consequence.

### Domain Building — Medium
A long-form stewardship mode for estates, settlements, households, guilds, companies, military holdings, and institutions where money, people, logistics, legitimacy, and accumulated decisions shape what you build.

### War Frontier — Hard
A harder military and borderlands mode where raids, patrols, armies, command decisions, wounds, supplies, and faction pressure matter.

### Harsh Survival
A punishing mode where resources are scarce, travel is dangerous, injuries linger, and bad decisions carry forward.

## Story Structure Modes

### Heist
Uses a plan-and-execute rhythm: casing, preparation, roles, target knowledge, access, timing, contingencies, execution, and consequences. Good planning matters; the mode does not force a complication merely because the genre expects one.

### Mystery
Centers investigation, evidence, testimony, contradictions, uncertainty, and earned conclusions. Clues should be discoverable through appropriate methods rather than handed out as omniscient narration.

## Narration Pace Modes

- **Brisk** — moves efficiently through low-value connective material while preserving decisions and consequences.
- **Slow Burn** — gives relationships, tension, investigation, atmosphere, and gradual developments more room before payoff.
- **Whirlwind** — favors rapid escalation and compression while still respecting established state and player agency.

## Narration Genres

The configured narration genres are:

- Absurd Comedy
- Cosmic Horror
- Cozy Comedy
- Dark Comedy
- Drama
- Fairy Tale
- General Horror
- Grimdark
- Gritty Action
- Heroic Action
- Mythic
- Psychological Horror
- Romance
- Satire
- Screwball Comedy
- Supernatural Horror
- Swashbuckling
- Tragedy

Narration genres change presentation, emphasis, and scene texture. They do not grant NPCs knowledge, rewrite canon, remove consequences, or replace the rules of the active campaign mode.
