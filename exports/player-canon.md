# Arkenfell

Arkenfell is the setting reference for this Voyage world. This wiki is intended to help players understand the people, places, institutions, cultures, history, and terminology their characters may reasonably encounter.

## What belongs here

This public wiki contains **player-facing canon**: established information that can be safely read without revealing hidden motives, unresolved mysteries, secret histories, or campaign-specific outcomes.

The wiki is a reference source, not a replacement for Voyage's own World Lore and structured world data. Voyage still needs the relevant setting knowledge encoded inside the game so the narrator can retrieve and use it during play.

## Knowledge levels

Articles may identify how widely information is known inside the setting. A fact being available to players in this wiki does not mean every NPC knows it.

- **Common** — broadly known by ordinary people in the relevant culture or region.
- **Regional** — broadly known within a particular realm, region, or community.
- **Educated** — likely known to educated people, officials, scholars, clergy, or specialists.
- **Specialist** — requires a profession, discipline, institution, or focused study.
- **Restricted** — known to a limited group but not treated as a GM secret.

## Canon and campaigns

The wiki records setting canon. Individual Voyage campaigns can diverge from that baseline through player choices, quest outcomes, deaths, succession changes, political decisions, and other state changes. Those campaign outcomes belong in the campaign's game state rather than being silently rewritten into general setting canon.

# Eldaven

Eldaven is the mapped continent currently at the center of Arkenfell's known political world. It contains old kingdoms, merchant republics, religious states, mage-ruled powers, frontier regions, and many smaller cultures whose interests overlap through trade, war, faith, marriage, scholarship, and travel.

The major powers most relevant to continental politics are the Kingdom of Valenreach, the Dreadmark Dominion, the Tide Empire, the Lythael Elven Dominion, the Solmire Concord, the Solarian Theocracy, and the Arcanum Ascendancy.

Valenreach and the Dreadmark Dominion exist in a cold war: agents, scouts, merchants, diplomats, scholars, mercenaries, and proxies may compete even when open war has not begun. Other powers have their own rivalries and suspicions, but hostility between states should not be mistaken for permanent war.

Eldaven is only the currently mapped continental focus of Arkenfell. Lands beyond it are not assumed empty or uncivilized.

# Kingdom of Valenreach

The Kingdom of Valenreach is a major royal power of Eldaven and the primary political frame for many Arkenfell stories. King Aldric and Queen Ceralie rule from Highcourt Armathen; Crown Prince Rowan is the established public heir, and Princess Elira is the younger princess.

Valenreach presents itself as lawful, wealthy, fortified, and legitimate. Noble estates, mage institutions, abbeys, military musters, river trade, rural villages, Crown roads, and royal schools all bind the kingdom together, although royal authority is felt very differently in the capital, old noble territories, and distant villages.

Its armed strength includes ordinary foot soldiers alongside spellswords, battle-mages, paladins, and other trained forces. The kingdom is especially known for disciplined magical institutions and for treating unusual magic as a matter that may carry political as well as practical consequences.

Ancient dragons are said to dwell in the northern mountain ranges. Such claims are part of Valenreach's public folklore and frontier reputation, not proof that every old draconic tale is true.

# The Dreadmark Dominion

The Dreadmark Dominion lies east of Valenreach and consists of numerous cities, fortified settlements, military roads, extraction sites, and controlled territories centered on its capital, Vhal Kareth.

The Malrec bloodline has ruled the Dominion for nearly four centuries. Its state is built around strict hierarchy, military order, forced labor, and institutionalized slavery. Authority is deliberately visible through legions, overseers, records, controlled movement, and the constant distinction between those who command and those who must obey.

Dreadmark surpasses Valenreach in sheer military numbers, fielding vast infantry forces and war-beasts, although it does not possess the same depth of trained mages. The two powers remain locked in a cold war in which border pressure, agents, military preparation, and proxy interests matter even without open invasion.

# The Tide Empire

The Tide Empire is one of Eldaven's oldest surviving realms and a long-standing northern ally of Valenreach. Empress Lysara Marovyn rules from Marovyn Tidemark, an ancient capital built around a great river mouth and fortified harbor.

The empire controls fortified river cities, northern coastal strongholds, and important trade routes linking inland kingdoms to the northern seas. Imperial fleets guard its waterways and coasts, while dragonrider orders patrol the skies above strategically important territory.

Its political strength comes from age, continuity, naval discipline, provincial government, clan and local traditions, and the expectation that imperial institutions will continue functioning through difficult winters and changing rulers. The Tide Empire is widely treated as a balancing power in northern diplomacy rather than merely a military ally.

# The Lythael Elven Dominion

The Lythael Elven Dominion lies far to the south beyond the central human kingdoms, across vast ancient forests, hidden cities, and carefully guarded woodland roads.

It is one of the oldest surviving realms in the known world. The Lythael royal line has ruled for more than a thousand years within a society traditionally guided by powerful noble matriarchs and long-lived houses whose political memory reaches far beyond a human lifetime.

Lythael maintains formal relations with neighboring powers but favors patience, stability, etiquette, and long-term strategy over open displays of force. Its courts often regard haste as poor judgment, and many within the Dominion quietly consider their civilization older and more refined than the younger human realms.

# The Solmire Concord

The Solmire Concord is a wealthy western merchant republic whose influence rests on sea trade, grain exports, convoy agreements, insurance, tariffs, credit, and powerful trade families rather than a royal crown.

Its capital, Solmire Argentport, is governed through merchant houses, council and senate blocs, convoy interests, commercial witnesses, and contracts capable of shaping foreign policy without an army crossing a border.

The Concord is officially neutral in many international conflicts, but neutrality is an active policy rather than political absence. Solmire merchants, brokers, shipowners, and councillors pursue access, stability, information, and profit wherever continental politics affect trade.

---

# The Solarian Theocracy

The Solarian Theocracy lies southeast of the central trade roads and is ruled by a High Synod of temple-lords rather than a royal dynasty.

Its cities and institutions are built around sanctuaries, pilgrimage roads, healing houses, doctrine, relic custody, and public religious service. The realm is smaller than the greatest kingdoms, but its priests, healers, sanctuaries, and temple alliances give it influence far beyond its borders.

Foreign rulers may seek Solarian approval or mediation during succession disputes, religious controversies, public unrest, and dangerous magical crises. This influence is significant, but it does not automatically give the Theocracy legal authority inside another sovereign realm.

# The Arcanum Ascendancy

The Arcanum Ascendancy is an independent mage-ruled state built around ancient academies, arcane towers, research institutions, classification systems, and controlled magical infrastructure rather than noble bloodlines.

Political authority is concentrated in powerful archmages and the Ascendancy Conclave. High Archon Selvaris Tyth serves as the public head of that system from Vaelthryn Spire-City.

The Ascendancy rarely relies on large foreign armies to project influence. Scholars, magical arbitration, relic research, classification expertise, containment practice, and access to specialist institutions allow it to shape disputes across borders.

Magic in the Ascendancy is treated as something to study, classify, test, regulate, and sometimes contain. That does not make every mage a state servant, but unusual magical capability can attract institutional attention.

# Edgar Vaust

Lord Steward Edgar Vaust is the royal household steward of Highcourt Armathen in Valenreach. He is a senior figure within the royal household and is closely associated with the orderly running of the palace and the stability of the Crown's household.

## Role

Edgar serves as **Lord Steward of Highcourt Armathen**. His office belongs to the royal household rather than to an outside political faction or private noble household.

## Character

Edgar is known for a kind, grounding, and steady presence. He is the sort of court figure who brings calm and practical order to difficult situations rather than seeking drama or personal power.

## Appearance

Edgar has a shorter, stocky build and a bearded appearance. His presentation should read as that of an established senior household official rather than a military scout or field officer.

## Loyalty

Edgar's loyalty is to the kingdom and the royal household he serves. His steadiness and discretion are part of that loyalty, not signs of divided allegiance.

# Highcourt Armathen

Highcourt Armathen is the royal palace of Valenreach and the seat of its royal household.

## Royal Household

The palace is administered as a royal household in its own right, with offices responsible for maintaining order, continuity, ceremony, and the practical functioning of court life.

The **Lord Steward of Highcourt Armathen is Edgar Vaust**, who serves as the royal household steward.

## Role in Valenreach

Highcourt Armathen is a central location of royal life in Valenreach. It should be understood first as the palace and household of the Crown, rather than as an ordinary noble estate or purely military seat.

# Arcana

Arcana are named magical affinities and disciplines used throughout Arkenfell. A character possessing more Arcana has greater breadth of magical access; that count does not by itself measure mastery.

Skill with an Arcana represents training, control, reliability, and depth. Someone with a single well-developed Arcana may be a more capable specialist than someone with several affinities but little practice. Arcane Lore and other knowledge skills are not additional Arcana.

Common Arcana are not extraordinary merely because they are magical. Trained mages may possess several. Rare and Legendary Arcana are different: their rarity, political implications, religious interpretation, danger, or unusual applications can attract institutional and faction attention.

Arcana may become apparent through childhood talent, training, testing, study, instinct, crisis, or later life. An Arcana does not determine a person's morality, personality, profession, bloodline, prophecy, or destiny.

Rare and Legendary affinities are not ordinary beginner selections. When such power emerges, its existence should follow established events, discoveries, or other genuine causes.

# Power Arcana

Power Arcana is a Willpower-based magical discipline centered on amplifying force beyond natural limits through raw magical pressure, enhanced physical output, kinetic reinforcement, and impact escalation.

## What it does

Power Arcana strengthens an action. It can reinforce a strike, leap, defense, spell impact, or other immediate exertion so that the action carries greater force than it otherwise could.

It does **not** replace the underlying skill or control required to perform that action.

## What it is not

Power Arcana is not the same thing as ordinary physical strength. **Might** represents raw physical strength, while relevant **Fighting skills** represent weapon technique and trained combat execution.

Power Arcana is also a distinct Arcana and should not be confused with **Force Arcana** merely because both can involve force in ordinary language.

# The Divine Powers of Eldaven

The peoples of Eldaven recognize many divine powers rather than one universal church. Nations and enduring cultures often claim a benefactor god connected to founding law, survival, rulership, identity, or sacred tradition, while ordinary people may pray to several gods over the course of their lives.

Arkenfell describes gods through **Moral/Theological Standing** rather than a fixed cosmic alignment. Common standings include Revered Benevolent, Revered Ambivalent, Accepted Necessary, Accepted Ambivalent, Controversial Patron, Feared but Recognized, and Forbidden Malignant. These categories describe how cultures and institutions treat worship; they are not an objective rule that every individual agrees with.

Among the major Eldaven powers, Valenreach honors Ardoval, the Oath-Crowned; the Tide Empire honors Maelthar, the Storm-Bearer; the Dreadmark Dominion honors Dravok, the Iron Will; the Lythael Elven Dominion honors Lythirien, the Root of Memory; the Solmire Concord honors Serrava, the Open Hand; the Solarian Theocracy honors Solcaris, the Unblinking Sun; and the Arcanum Ascendancy honors Myrathis, the Veiled Flame.

# Ardoval, the Oath-Crowned

Ardoval is Valenreach's benefactor god of oaths, crowns, rightful rule, inheritance, courts, sworn duty, guardianship, and noble burden.

His Moral/Theological Standing in Valenreach is **Revered Benevolent**. His symbol is a crowned sword laid across an open oath-ring. Kings, judges, knights, nobles, officers, and heirs invoke him in rites and promises connected to coronations, verdicts, contracts, marriages, guardianship, and battlefield vows.

Ardoval's worship is closely associated with the idea that power creates obligations rather than merely privilege. Critics of his institutions warn that devotion to lawful continuity can also protect old privilege when law is treated as holier than mercy.
